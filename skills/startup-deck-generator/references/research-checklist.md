# Research checklist

The goal is not a dossier. It is the four or five signals that justify a specific
change to a specific slide. Stop when the diff is well-founded — additional research
past that point makes the tailoring slower without making it better.

Public sources only. This skill is meant to work for a founder with nothing but a
browser. If a CRM or enrichment tool happens to be connected, use it as a bonus; never
build a step that requires one.

---

## Order of work

Roughly descending value per minute spent.

### 1. Whatever the founder already has

Ask first, search second. A first-call note, an intro email, a partner's reply, a
friend at the firm — any of these outrank public research, because they're specific to
this conversation rather than to the fund in general. Founders routinely fail to
mention them because they don't seem like "research."

### 2. The fund's own thesis writing

Their blog, memos, published theses, LP-facing summaries. This is the highest-value
public source, particularly for sector specialists, because funds write to attract
dealflow — the writing describes the exact frame they will evaluate you in.

Read for vocabulary as much as content. The words they use for the space are the words
the deck should use.

### 3. The portfolio page

The thesis as revealed by behaviour rather than stated by copy. Two questions:

- **Which companies are adjacent to this one?** Adjacency is usually an asset — it
  means the fund already believes the market thesis and you skip a step.
- **What's the pattern?** Stage, sector concentration, geography, whether they appear
  to lead. Patterns are more reliable than any single data point.

### 4. Recent investments — the last 12–18 months specifically

Stated strategy and current behaviour diverge more often than not, and the recent
behaviour is what governs. Announced rounds tell you actual stage, actual check size,
and whether they led. Press releases and the fund's own announcements are enough; paid
databases add precision you probably don't need for a slide diff.

### 5. The target partner

Frequently more determinative than the fund (see `investor-archetypes.md`). Look for:
prior operating background, published writing and podcast appearances, board seats, and
what they personally have invested in recently.

Where partner and fund diverge, weight the partner. They're the one who has to carry
it internally.

### 6. Fund size and vintage, if easily available

Shapes check size and ownership expectation, which shapes the ask slide. A first fund
behaves differently from a fourth; a fund three years into deployment behaves
differently from one that just closed.

Public filings, press announcements, or the fund's own materials. If it takes more than
a few minutes, skip it and note the gap — this is useful context, not a blocker.

### 7. Conference talks and podcast appearances

The same material as their written thesis with the hedges removed. Worth it when a
first-pass read of the thesis left the actual filter unclear.

## What you can't get publicly — flag, don't guess

Naming these keeps the tailoring honest and tells the founder what to ask on the call.

- **Current dry powder and pacing.** Whether they have room for a new position this
  quarter is invisible from outside, and it decides more outcomes than thesis fit.
- **Internal politics.** Which partner is ascendant, who owes whom a favour, whether
  the last deal in this sector went badly.
- **Real check-size flexibility.** Published ranges are marketing.
- **Whether they've already seen a competitor.** Sometimes inferable from the
  portfolio, usually not — a fund can have diligenced three companies in your space
  and invested in none.
- **Why they passed on something similar.** Occasionally public, usually not, and the
  public version is rarely the real one.

Anything material and unknowable goes in the strategy doc as a question for the call,
and anything asserted in the deck that depends on it gets a `[REVIEW: ...]` flag. See
`honest-tailoring.md`.

---

## A note on what this skill does not check

v1 deliberately does not analyse portfolio conflicts, ownership-math feasibility, or
stage drift as gating checks. That means the research above can surface a fund that
already holds a direct competitor, and nothing in the workflow will stop the deck
being built.

If the research does surface something like that, say so in the strategy doc. It isn't
a formal step, but a founder who learns after the meeting that this fund led their
closest competitor's Series A is worse off than one who heard it from you — and a
sentence costs nothing.
