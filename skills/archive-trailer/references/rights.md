# Rights checklist

Work through this before anything is published. Put the results in CONTEXT.md.

## Footage
- movingimagearchive.com says its material is public domain. Treat that as a claim to verify.
- US films published before 1931 are public domain (the cutoff moves forward each January).
- 1931 to 1977: public domain only if published without a copyright notice, or the copyright was
  not renewed. Many industrial and government films qualify; check each source.
- After 1977: public domain only if a US federal government work or explicitly dedicated.
- "Year unknown" means unverified. List those picks for checking.
- Archive.org items tagged public domain are often user uploads with self-asserted licenses
  (copyrighted cartoons and TV episodes turned up under that tag). Don't rely on the tag.
- NASA footage is generally not copyrighted, but NASA's media guidelines forbid implying
  endorsement, restrict the NASA insignia, and apply to identifiable NASA personnel.

## Music
- Clear the composition, the recording and any arrangement separately (`music.md`). A "public
  domain" label on a band transcription usually covers only the performance.
- Musopen recordings rest on the uploader's warranty and often don't name the performer: a
  reasonable basis, but weaker than a federal recording. Say which basis you're relying on.

## In the frame
- Third-party logos, brand signs and burned-in title cards: cut them.
- Identifiable people: flag them. Publicity rights and endorsement rules can apply.
- Real public figures (interviews, name captions): cut them. In a promo they read as endorsement.
- Visible copyright notices in a frame ("Copyright MCMXLII" on a title card) are also evidence
  about the source's status: that film was published with notice, so check its renewal.
- Someone else's product under a card about your subject: cut it (it reads as the product).

## On the cards
- Every card is a claim. Tie it to the source (the book, the event page) and get the owner's OK.
- Don't name real companies or people as if they endorse the subject. A historical fact from the
  source material is fine; an implied endorsement is not.
- Dates, times and prices: only when confirmed, and consistent with every public page.
